Three rows on one shared horizontal axis, and each answers a different question that the numbers alone answer badly: ARRANGEMENT where the sections are and how long they run BANDS what each bar is made of — the curve the boundaries were cut from REPEATS which sections are the same material The third row is the one that could not be drawn before. A section used to be a duration and a kick-energy float, so "these two are the same chorus" was not a claim the analyser could make; it now measures segment types, and a lane per type puts the song's own form on screen in one glance. Deliberately no colour-coded categories. The console has one accent hue, and inventing six more to key section types would (a) collide with the status colours, which mean failed and blocked, and (b) put identity in colour alone. So one hue does one job — magnitude, on the ordered frequency bands, light to dark — and identity is carried by direct labels and the repeats lanes. That is also why the band stack has a 2px gap between its segments: adjacent steps of a sequential ramp are close by construction, and the gap, the legend and the table below are what keep them separable. Every number rendered here is measured.
