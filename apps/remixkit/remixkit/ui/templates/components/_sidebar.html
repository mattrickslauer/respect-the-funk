{% from "components/_macros.html" import layer %} {# The console's one piece of standing furniture. It replaces a 50px topbar and a ~28px warning banner — 78px off the top of every page, on a laptop whose usable viewport after browser chrome is about 780px. That is 10% of the budget this whole layout was designed around (see console.css), spent on five links and a line of deployment state. Moving both into a column trades width, of which there is 1440px and to spare, for height, of which there is never enough. The tree is contextual: the roster is always listed, and the artist you are inside expands to their identity, their songs and their kits. `ui.routes._nav` explains why only the active branch expands. What that buys is the hop it removes — song to sibling song, or song to kit, used to be two navigations through the artist page and is now one click on something already on screen. Everything below the brand is a `.navgroup`: a head, and a `.navbody` the head folds away. Songs and kits used to run together as one undifferentiated list of sub-items under an artist — a song and a kit are different kinds of thing, and the rail said so nowhere — so each is now a named section with its own count and its own twisty, and so are the roster, the open artist, and the four screens that are not a record. An artist with a dozen songs no longer pushes their kits off the bottom of the rail: fold Songs and the kits are back on screen. Both kinds of collapsing — the whole rail, and one section of it — are CSS keyed off an attribute on ``, set before first paint by the inline script in the layout head. State on `` rather than in this markup is deliberate: the rail is re-rendered out-of-band on every mutation (see `_layout.html`'s `#rk-live`), and anything somebody had folded shut would spring open on their next save if the state lived in here. #}
