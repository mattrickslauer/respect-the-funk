Organic UGC is the only channel with non-negative economics

Fan-made video is the one distribution mechanism that isn't a loss per unit. But it is unpredictable by nature — the top 10% of songs take 96% of all TikTok creations. You cannot buy your way into it, and no vendor can promise it.

So the winning strategy is volume, not optimisation

When outcomes are lottery-shaped, the edge isn't a better ticket — it's more tickets at a radically lower cost per ticket, with the plumbing correct so a win actually pays. That is a cost-structure problem. That is what we built.

04
The product

One release in. A remix economy out.

Five layers, each of which works standalone and compounds with the others.

1 · Kit Generator

Upload an owned master, set the hook window. We orchestrate multiple generative video, image and audio models into a pack of vertical, remix-ready templates — in minutes, per release.

2 · Composite Clips ◆

Fans make videos together. Pick a format, record a slot, invite friends by link to fill the rest. We composite the segments into one finished clip. Every composite requires an invite — this is the growth engine.

3 · Creator Marketplace

Every contributor gets a tracked link, a leaderboard position and a share of the label's reward pool. Fans become a measurable, compensable distribution channel.

4 · Provenance & Disclosure

Every asset carries a hash-verified manifest embedded inside the media file — which model made it, from which prompt, for which release, with what lineage. Disclosure terms are enforced by the funnel, not requested politely.

5 · Cost & Analytics Ledger

Per-release, per-asset, per-tenant cost attribution from day one, exported warehouse-ready. Labels see exactly what each campaign cost and what it returned. Nobody else in this category shows the bill.

What we deliberately don't sell

  • Virality guarantees
  • Stream-count attribution (technically impossible)
  • Bought engagement or bot networks

Each is dead for a sourced reason in our research. Selling them is how this category earned its reputation.

05
The growth engine

Every video made needs a friend to finish it.

Composite clips make invitation a structural requirement of creation, not a marketing ask.

Why it works where seeding fails

Paid seeding stops the day the budget stops — our research found no rigorous evidence it ever cascades. A friend asking you to fill slot 2 of 3 is social obligation, which converts without spend and produces content that is organic by construction.

It targets the one proven predictor

The strongest academic finding on TikTok virality is that network position predicts spread — the audio doesn't. A composite clip enters three follower graphs instead of one, by design. We can't make a song good enough to spread. We can multiply the graphs it enters.

Unit economics

Expensive AI runs once per release.
Cheap assembly runs once per fan.

All-in cost per participating fan, as a release scales

Illustrative model at stated assumptions — $12 one-time generation per release kit, ~$0.02 per composite (CPU + storage + delivery). Not a measured figure; the shape is the claim, and it follows directly from the architecture.

The architectural claim

A fan action never triggers a model call. Generation is amortised across every fan who ever remixes that release; assembly is deterministic ffmpeg — no GPU, seconds, fractions of a cent.

What this means commercially

  • Gross margin improves with virality instead of degrading.
  • A release going viral is a storage read, not a compute event.
  • Content-addressable reuse cuts the dominant cost line further on repeat generations.
  • Idle infrastructure cost is ~$0 — scale-to-zero throughout.

This is the difference between a product that gets expensive when it works and one that gets cheaper.

07
Built to scale

Designed for a 1000× spike with no redeploy.

Virality is spiky and unpredictable by nature. The architecture assumes that rather than hoping against it.

Generate once, remix infinitely

Expensive generative work is per-release; cheap deterministic work is per-fan. No fan action ever reaches a model.

Nothing large transits the server

Browser talks to object storage directly via presigned URLs. The API moves JSON only — request volume is decoupled from bandwidth entirely.

Long work is always queued

Every generation and composite is an idempotent, keyed background job. HTTP handlers stay under 200ms, always.

Serve from cache, not origin

Finished kits and composites are immutable and CDN-fronted. At peak the hot path is storage; compute barely participates.

Tenant is a partition key everywhere

Storage prefix, database row, metric, provenance manifest. Multi-tenancy is cheap on day one and near-impossible to retrofit.

Stateless, scale-to-zero

State lives only in Postgres and object storage. Idle cost ≈ $0; scale-out is horizontal and unbounded.

Deliberately deferred

Sharding, multi-region, Kubernetes, custom schedulers, realtime sockets. None are needed before the above saturates. Writing down what we're not building is how the roadmap stays honest.

Provenance is native, not bolted on

Every generated asset and every fan composite carries a hash-verified manifest with full lineage — finished clip back to the exact models and prompts. Verifiable by anyone, offline, from the file itself.

Why this is investable

Five things that are hard to copy.

01

Margins that improve with success

Per-fan marginal cost approaches a floor of cents with no model call. Competitors who generate per-user hit a wall exactly when they start working. This is a structural advantage, not a discount.

02

A real viral loop, instrumented

Composites cannot be made alone. Invitation is a requirement of the product, not a growth hack bolted onto it — and K-factor is measured from day one.

03

Regulatory tailwind

Disclosure-by-default and embedded provenance are exactly what the FTC is now enforcing and what AI-media rules are converging on. Incumbent practice is the violation.

04

A research moat that says no

Thirteen research pillars and ~2,440 sourced claims underpin this product — and most of them killed an idea. We know what doesn't work here with citations, which is why we're not spending the next two years discovering it.

05

A first tenant that is also a proving ground

Respect the Funk is customer zero. Every feature ships against a real catalogue and a real release before it ships to anyone else. The product cannot drift into demo-ware.

The honest risk

Fan adoption of collaborative creation is the assumption to test, and we have not yet tested it at scale. Breakout remains a lottery — we sell cost structure and compliance, never outcomes.

Stated plainly because a deck that hides this is the one to worry about.

Use of funds

Where investment goes.

Allocation for an 18-month runway to a validated K-factor and a paying label cohort.

■ Engineering & platform scale — 40%

A small senior team. Compositing pipeline throughput, job orchestration, CDN delivery, and the multi-tenant control plane. This is the line that turns the architecture on page 8 from a design into an operating system.

■ Go-to-market — 18%

Independent-label partnerships and catalogue onboarding. Sales here is a small number of high-trust relationships, not a paid-acquisition machine — the product's own loop is the acquisition channel.

■ Generative compute & storage — 15%

Model spend and object storage. Deliberately the smallest variable line by design — generate-once amortisation and content-addressable reuse are what keep it there.

■ Creator marketplace & rewards — 15%

Priming reward pools so the first cohorts of fans have something real to earn, plus payment infrastructure. This is the cold-start cost of a two-sided marketplace, spent knowingly and capped.

■ Rights, compliance & legal — 12%

Entertainment counsel, licensing posture, FTC disclosure framework and the provenance audit trail. In this category, compliance is a feature customers buy — not a cost centre.
