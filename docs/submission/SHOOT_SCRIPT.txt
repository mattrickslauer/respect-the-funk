WHAT THE VIDEO HAS TO DO, IN ORDER
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    1. Say what this is and why anyone should care.        (first 25 seconds)
    2. Make precision the thing everything depends on.
    3. Show the machine is real and runs itself.
    4. Land the one thing only CockroachDB can do.
    5. Tell them where to go.

Earlier drafts opened on internal machinery — a budget raise, a row count — and
never answered (1) at all. A viewer who doesn't know what the product is has no
reason to care that the shortlist is a filtered vector search.

So the opening is now the label's actual problem: finding the few hundred
people who might play a record, and writing to each of them personally, which
is weeks of work that most labels simply never do. *So most records just
disappear.* That's the line that buys the next two and a half minutes.

The autonomous budget raise moved from the cold open to two-thirds in. It's a
much stronger beat once the viewer knows what's spending the money.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NO NUMBERS. ANYWHERE.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

There is not a single digit in the spoken script — checked programmatically.
There should not be one in the visuals either.

Nobody remembers that the index holds forty-three thousand rows, and nobody
was going to be convinced by it. Worse, a number is the only kind of claim a
viewer can catch you on. Counts, costs and row totals are out of the voice and
out of the overlays.

What replaces them is the point of the next section: pictures that make a
concept obvious in two seconds. A query plan held on screen for eight seconds
is a wall of text that reads as "technical" and communicates nothing. A
diagram of the same idea lands before the sentence describing it finishes.

RANK 2 · THE TIME SLIDER                                  (over the replay beat)
    A valuation card with a few bars on it. A timeline runs underneath with a

    and the bars visibly change to different values — then a tick appears:

    This is the money shot. It explains AS OF SYSTEM TIME to someone who has

RANK 3 · THE MAP                                        (over the residency beat)
    One table icon. Two pins, Virginia and Ireland, with a line from the table

RANK 4 · THE CHAIN                                       (over the runtime beat)
    A central database pulsing; agents light up one after another as the pulse
    reaches them — never agent-to-agent, always through the middle. Then half
    the agents go black and the pulse simply routes around them.
    Immediately after: two identical worker icons reach for the same row. One
    gets a key, the other's hand bounces off it.

RANK 5 · THE INDEX FILLING UP                       (over the "never finished")
    The same dot field from Rank 1, with new facts landing on records as small

    texture rather than explanation, and it sets up the replay beat: things
    keep changing, which is why the old numbers are gone.

RANK 6 · THE TOKEN                                        (over the reply line)
    An envelope leaves carrying a coloured tag. It comes back with the same
    tag. The tag snaps it onto the right thread in a list.

TEXTURE, NOT INFORMATION

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SHOOTING IT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Desk, chest-up, eyeline on the lens. One frame throughout — the visuals are
the variety, and a moving camera fights them.

Audio is the product. Mic within a foot, off-axis, out of frame. Record 30
seconds of room tone before you start; the editor needs it at every seam and
can't make it later.

One take, then two more. Don't shoot it beat by beat — the pacing is relative
and a stitched take loses it.

Four pauses that have to be performed. A pause made in the edit is silence; a
pause you performed is a thought.

    after "most records just disappear"
    after "you're spam"
    after "the token can"
    after "Not a copy of them. Them."      (count two)

The shortest sentence in each stretch is the one that lands. Slow it by half:
"Spindle does it." · "You're spam." · "The token can." · "Them."

The last line is a dare, not a plea. Say "go and break it" like you mean it.

Never say "as you can see."

THE THREE THINGS NOT TO SAY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Short list, because with no numbers in the script most of the exposure is gone.

1  DON'T IMPLY THE HOSTED DEMO IS MULTI-REGION. It runs single-region. The
   residency beat is shot on an ephemeral cluster from committed Terraform,
   and the submission text should say so in one plain sentence. It's the one
   claim a judge can check without leaving their seat.

2  DON'T SAY A CONTACT IS EUROPEAN. Every contact route we hold with a country
   on it reads US. "This one is physically in Ireland" describes the row on
   screen, and that's where it stops.

3  DON'T CLAIM BEDROCK. Implemented, unreachable on this account, both walls
   measured. Embeddings are OpenAI. AWS is covered by Lambda, S3, SES and
   Secrets Manager, all real and in use.
