Release n+1 should cost less and land harder than release n. That only happens if something accumulates in between — the relationships, the audience you actually reached, and what you learned the last four times. This is the thing that accumulates.

What it does

It researches your artists, and it does not stop. Not a one-off scrape — a queue that keeps going across every platform, including the ones your artist has no account on. Fan activity is content about them whether they take part or not.

It builds an index of everyone who can help. Creators, playlist runners, programmers, writers — with how you found each one, and where the relationship actually stands. Two of your artists can never work the same creator at once, because the database refuses it rather than trusting anyone to remember.

It runs every channel at the same time. UGC, radio, curators, press. One engine, a different playbook each — adding a channel is configuration, not a new product. Nothing sends without you approving it.

It learns from replies, not from stream counts. Streams are confounded by playlist adds, algorithmic push, seasonality and your own campaigns firing at once. A reply is fast and clean. The fast signal is what teaches it; the slow one only audits whether the fast one was lying.

Why you would trust it

It shows its work. Every single thing it believes records what that belief stands on, back to the page it was read from or the person who said it. Retract one fact and everything built on top of it goes stale on its own.

It refuses to guess. When your own answer and a public record disagree about who owns a master, it stops and asks you. Rights are exactly where quietly picking a winner costs real money.

It tells you what it skipped. When an artist hits their daily budget, the work it dropped is written down. A system that truncates silently reads as “we covered everything” when it did not.
