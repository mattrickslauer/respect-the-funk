# Pillar 1: Social Platform Publishing APIs

Research for the "Losing Sleep" (Hallow Youth / Respect the Funk) marketing engine — a system to programmatically publish and track content across many social accounts, evaluated for cost, rate limits, and approval friction. Researched July 2026.

---

## Bottom line / recommendation

**Build vs. buy: buy a middleware aggregator for the long tail of platforms; go direct only where the economics or account count justify it.**

- **Meta (Instagram/Facebook/Threads) and YouTube are cheap and low-friction to integrate directly** if you only ever publish to accounts your own business owns and manages: Meta's Graph API grants "Standard Access" (no App Review, no Business Verification) for self-owned accounts, and both APIs are free to call ([Meta App Review docs](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/app-review/); [YouTube Data API overview](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/getting-started)). This is the strongest case for direct build, since Respect the Funk owns the accounts.
- **TikTok is the highest-friction platform**: unaudited apps can only post to 5 users/24h in `SELF_ONLY` (private) visibility — functionally useless for marketing until you pass TikTok's audit, which requires demonstrating ToS compliance ([TikTok Content Sharing Guidelines](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/content-sharing-guidelines)). Budget real lead time (weeks) for this regardless of build-vs-buy.
- **X (Twitter) is now pay-per-use** ($0.015/post created, $0.005/post read, no free tier as of 2026) ([X API pricing](https://docs.x.com/x-api/getting-started/pricing)) — cheap for our low posting volume, likely $20–50/month even across dozens of accounts, but X's automation rules explicitly forbid posting "duplicative or substantially similar" content across multiple accounts you operate ([X Automation Rules, cited via secondary source](https://opentweet.io/blog/twitter-automation-rules-2026)), which directly constrains a "same clip everywhere" strategy.
- **For the long tail (Snapchat, Reddit, Pinterest, Bluesky, and as a hedge on Instagram/TikTok/YouTube App Review timelines), an aggregator is the better first move.** At our likely scale (10–50 accounts), **Ayrshare or Late/Zernio cost $50–800/month** and already hold the App Review approvals for every major platform, eliminating weeks of review latency ([Ayrshare pricing](https://www.ayrshare.com/pricing/); Late/Zernio graduated pricing, see §6). Direct integration only pays off once you're managing 200+ accounts, where per-account aggregator fees start to exceed engineering cost — and even then, TikTok's audit and Meta's App Review still gate you regardless of who's calling the API. Snapchat is a real, if secondary, channel here: Snap's **Public Profile API** does support organic Stories/Spotlight posting (not invite-gated like Discover), and Ayrshare has already integrated it (§5).
- **Reddit is now effectively closed to casual/free automation** — Reddit closed *all* self-service API access, commercial and non-commercial alike, in November 2025 under its new "Responsible Builder Policy"; every developer must apply and be approved before writing code, and commercial terms run roughly $0.24/1,000 calls with a ~$12,000/year minimum ([Responsible Builder Policy — Reddit Help](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/42728983564564-Responsible-Builder-Policy); [octolens.com Reddit API pricing](https://octolens.com/blog/reddit-api-pricing)). Deprioritize Reddit for this campaign unless doing manual/low-volume posting.
- **Recommended path:** start with an aggregator (Ayrshare or Late) to get Instagram/TikTok/YouTube/X/Threads/Pinterest/Bluesky live in days instead of weeks, in parallel file for Meta Standard Access + TikTok audit directly (both are free), and re-evaluate direct integration for Instagram/YouTube specifically once posting volume or cost crosses the aggregator's per-profile pricing curve.

## 1. Instagram / Meta

**Publishing mechanics.** Content Publishing uses a two-step Graph API flow: create a media container (`POST /{ig-user-id}/media`) then publish it (`POST /{ig-user-id}/media_publish`) ([Meta: Content Publishing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/content-publishing/)).

- **Reels**: `media_type=REELS` with a video URL; recommended cover 1080×1920 (9:16); the Graph API caps Reels at 90 seconds even though the app now allows up to 3 minutes natively ([Meta: Content Publishing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/content-publishing/)).
- **Carousels**: up to 10 images/videos, all cropped to the first item's aspect ratio; counts as a single post toward rate limits ([Meta: Content Publishing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/content-publishing/)).
- **Stories**: **can** be published via the API using `media_type=STORIES` on the same container/publish flow — this corrects outdated assumptions that Stories were unsupported ([Meta: Content Publishing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/content-publishing/)). Published Stories expire after 24 hours as normal.

**Permissions.** As of Meta's Jan 27, 2025 permission rename, the current scopes are `instagram_business_basic`, `instagram_business_content_publish`, `instagram_business_manage_messages`, `instagram_business_manage_comments` (the old `instagram_basic`/`instagram_content_publish` names are deprecated) ([Meta: Business Login for Instagram](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-instagram-login/business-login)).

**Account type & App Review — the critical nuance.** Instagram requires a professional (Business or Creator) account; personal accounts have no API access ([Meta: Instagram Platform overview](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/overview/)). Crucially, **App Review and Business Verification are only required if your app publishes to Instagram accounts you don't own or manage (Advanced Access)**. If the app only serves accounts your own business owns/manages (our case — Respect the Funk's own accounts), Meta grants **Standard Access automatically with no App Review** ([Meta: App Review — Instagram Platform](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/app-review/)). This is a major cost/timeline win for a direct-build path, provided we never need to publish to an artist's account we don't administer.

**Facebook Page requirement depends on login flow.** "Instagram API with Facebook Login" (the full-featured publishing path) requires the Instagram professional account to be linked to a Facebook Page. "Instagram API with Instagram Login" (Business Login for Instagram) does **not** require a linked Page, but is a lighter-weight flow ([Meta: Instagram API with Instagram Login](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-instagram-login/); [Meta: Instagram API with Facebook Login](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-facebook-login)). Publishing capability specifically should be verified per-flow before committing to an integration path — UNVERIFIED whether Instagram Login now fully supports content publishing at parity with Facebook Login as of mid-2026; historically publishing lived on the Facebook Login path.

**Rate limits — two official numbers, partially reconciled.** Verified by independent direct fetch of the Content Publishing page: *"Instagram accounts are limited to **100 API-published posts** within a 24-hour moving period"*, and — importantly — the same page separately states that **carousels are limited to 50 published posts within a 24-hour period** ([Meta: Content Publishing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/content-publishing/), verified by direct fetch). A carousel still counts as one post, but carousels carry their own tighter 50/24h sub-limit.

Meanwhile the machine-checkable **`content_publishing_limit` API reference** defines `quota_total` as *"the maximum number of IG Containers the app user can publish within the `quota_duration` time period (currently `50`)"* over an 86,400-second window ([Meta: content_publishing_limit reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-user/content_publishing_limit/)).

**Reconciliation:** the 50 figure demonstrably applies to carousels on the overview page, which likely explains part — but **not provably all** — of the gap; whether the reference page's blanket `quota_total: 50` reflects a stricter global cap or simply a stale doc is **UNVERIFIED**. Both figures supersede the legacy 25/day number still repeated in older third-party guides. **Engineering guidance: do not hardcode either number — query `GET /{IG_ID}/content_publishing_limit` at runtime** and plan capacity conservatively around 50/24h/account.

**Cost.** The Graph API itself is free to call. Business Verification (required only for Advanced Access / managing others' accounts) is a Meta compliance process, not a paid product, though it can require legal document submission and 2–4 week review timelines ([search synthesis of Meta App Review docs](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/app-review/)).

## 2. TikTok

**Two posting modes.** The Content Posting API supports **Direct Post** (`source=PULL_FROM_URL`, publishes immediately to the creator's profile with full caption/privacy controls) and **Upload** (chunked upload to TikTok's inbox as a draft the creator must manually finish and publish in-app) ([TikTok: Content Posting API Get Started](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/content-posting-api-get-started)).

**The unaudited-client wall.** This is TikTok's defining constraint for a marketing use case: **"Unaudited API Clients can allow up to 5 users to post in a 24 hour window"** and **"Unaudited API Clients can only post contents in `SELF_ONLY` viewership"** ([TikTok: Content Sharing Guidelines](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/content-sharing-guidelines)) — meaning unaudited posts are private-only and invisible to the public, useless for driving streams. To lift this, the API client must pass TikTok's audit verifying ToS compliance; TikTok also explicitly forbids apps from adding "any brand name, logo, watermark, other promotional branding" to shared content ([TikTok: Content Sharing Guidelines](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/content-sharing-guidelines)) — the commonly-repeated claim that unaudited posts get a visible watermark is **UNVERIFIED** against primary docs; the confirmed restriction is the private/`SELF_ONLY` visibility gate, not a watermark per se.

**Rate limits (post-audit) — CONFIRMED against primary docs.** A direct fetch of `developers.tiktok.com/doc/content-sharing-guidelines` confirms the per-creator posting cap verbatim: *"There is a limit on the number of posts that can be made to a creator account in a 24-hour window via Direct Post API. The upper limit may vary among creators (**typically around 15 posts per day/ creator account**)."* This cap is shared across all API clients posting to that creator. TikTok additionally imposes a **"24-hour active creator cap for each API client based on the usage estimates provided in the audit application form"** — i.e. your app's total concurrent-creator allowance is negotiated at audit time, not a published constant ([TikTok: Content Sharing Guidelines](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/content-sharing-guidelines), verified by direct fetch).

> **Correction / anti-stale note:** the widely-circulated figure of **25 videos/day per account is NOT supported by TikTok's primary docs** — the documented figure is ~15/day/creator. Earlier drafts of this report and several secondary guides ([getphyllo.com](https://www.getphyllo.com/post/tiktok-api-rate-limits-in-2026-quotas-errors-workarounds)) cite 25; treat that as **UNVERIFIED** and plan capacity against 15. The **6 requests/minute per user access token** figure also comes only from secondary sources and is **UNVERIFIED** against primary docs.

**Multi-account management.** A single developer app can technically manage unlimited TikTok accounts via independent OAuth tokens, but per-app aggregate limits apply across all managed accounts; guidance from secondary sources suggests partitioning across multiple app credentials once managing more than ~100 accounts ([TikTok API Rate Limits guide](https://www.getphyllo.com/post/tiktok-api-rate-limits-in-2026-quotas-errors-workarounds)) — **UNVERIFIED against primary TikTok docs**.

**Display API vs. Research API.** The **Display API** (default rate ~600 requests/minute, per secondary sources) exposes a user's own basic profile/video data for apps they've authorized. The **Research API** is restricted to vetted academic/non-profit researchers in specific regions (US, EEA, UK, Switzerland, Brazil), capped at 1,000 requests/day and ~100,000 records/day, and requires a ~4-week manual application — it is **not** usable for commercial marketing purposes ([TikTok: Research API FAQ](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/research-api-faq); [TikTok Research Tools](https://developers.tiktok.com/products/research-api/)).

**Business API vs. Content Posting API.** TikTok's **Marketing/Business API** is for paid ad campaign management (targeting, budgets, creative upload for ads); the **Content Posting API** is the separate, distinct product for organic posting ([TikTok for Business Organic API docs](https://business-api.tiktok.com/portal/docs/organic-api/v1.3)).

**Cost.** The Content Posting API itself is free; TikTok does not appear to charge for organic posting API access as of 2026 (**UNVERIFIED** — no evidence found of a paid tier, but no explicit "this is free" statement located in primary docs either).

## 3. X (Twitter)

X's pricing model **changed fundamentally as of February 6, 2026**: it moved from tiered subscriptions to **pay-per-use as the default for new developers** ([X API pricing docs](https://docs.x.com/x-api/getting-started/pricing)).

**Current pricing (pay-per-use, default for new developers):**
- **$0.015 per post created** ($0.20 if the post contains a link)
- **$0.005 per post read**, capped at 2M reads/month; **$0.001 per "owned read"** (authenticated users reading their own data)
- No subscription fee, no free tier; users buy credits and set spending limits ([X API pricing docs](https://docs.x.com/x-api/getting-started/pricing))

**Legacy tiers** (Basic $200/mo, Pro $5,000/mo, Enterprise ~$42,000/mo) are closed to new signups; X began auto-migrating remaining legacy Basic subscribers to pay-per-use on June 1, 2026 ([search synthesis, X API pricing coverage](https://www.wearefounders.uk/the-x-api-price-hike-a-blow-to-indie-hackers/)) — the Enterprise figure specifically is **UNVERIFIED against primary docs**, sourced from secondary comparison sites.

**Cost verdict for this campaign.** Pay-per-use is actually favorable for low-volume organic posting: e.g., 50 accounts × 1 post/day × 30 days = 1,500 posts/month ≈ **$22.50/month** in post-creation cost alone, dramatically cheaper than the old $200/month Basic floor. Media upload is supported under the same posting endpoints. **Worth it for this campaign** as a supplementary channel at that volume — but note the automation-rules constraint (§7) against posting duplicative content across multiple owned accounts, which limits a naive "blast the same clip to 50 X accounts" strategy.

## 4. YouTube

**Default quota.** Each Google Cloud project gets **10,000 units/day** combined for most endpoints, **plus separate daily buckets of 100 `search.list` calls and 100 `videos.insert` calls** ([Google: YouTube Data API Quota Calculator](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/determine_quota_cost); [Google: Quota and Compliance Audits](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/guides/quota_and_compliance_audits)).

**Upload cost changed significantly in late 2025 — CORRECTED.** A direct fetch of Google's live Quota Calculator page (confirmed "Last updated 2026-06-01 UTC") shows `videos.insert` now costs **1 quota unit per call**, governed by its own separate daily bucket of **100 calls/day** — independent of the shared 10,000-unit/day pool ([Google: YouTube Data API — Determine Quota Cost](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/determine_quota_cost), verified by direct fetch). This supersedes the "~100 units/call" figure previously reported here from secondary synthesis, and far supersedes the old ~1,600-units/call figure still circulating in stale guides. **Practical result: up to 100 video/Shorts uploads per day are available on a completely default, no-quota-increase-requested Google Cloud project.** `search.list` has an identical, separate 100-calls/day bucket.

**Other costs:** `videos.list` ≈ 1 unit/call; `thumbnails.set` ≈ 50 units/call ([Google: Quota Calculator](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/determine_quota_cost)) — treat as indicative, confirm against the live calculator tool.

**Quota increases.** Google requires completing the **"YouTube API Services - Audit and Quota Extension Form"**, a compliance audit process; the YouTube API Services team then reviews and contacts the developer ([Google: Quota and Compliance Audits](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/guides/quota_and_compliance_audits)). Whether quota increases carry a monetary cost is **UNVERIFIED** — no fee was mentioned in the docs found, and the process reads as an approval/compliance gate rather than a paid product.

**Cost.** The API itself is free to use within quota.

## 6. Aggregators / middleware

Comparing unified publishing APIs that already hold platform App Review approvals, against direct build.

**Does using an aggregator actually remove our App Review/audit burden? Confirmed, vendor by vendor:**
- **Ayrshare: yes.** Ayrshare publishes through its own already-approved developer apps; per Ayrshare's own material, "you no longer need approval or worry about the details of TikTok's evolving API" ([Ayrshare — Introducing TikTok Direct Publishing](https://www.ayrshare.com/introducing-tiktok-direct-publishing-analytics-and-commenting/)).
- **Blotato: yes.** "Blotato's own developer application has already been pre-approved by these platforms, so individual developers using Blotato can publish directly without going through their own app review cycles" — connect an account via standard OAuth and post the same day ([Blotato — TikTok API Pricing 2026](https://www.blotato.com/blog/tiktok-api-pricing)).
- **Late (getlate.dev): yes, confirmed.** Late's own TikTok integration docs state plainly: "no TikTok developer app approval process — just get your API key and start building"; Late "handles the TikTok side while your app authenticates with Late." This resolves the earlier open question in the pricing table below — Late does ride on its own pre-cleared developer apps, the same as Ayrshare/Blotato ([Late — TikTok API](https://getlate.dev/tiktok-api); [Late Docs — TikTok platform](https://docs.getlate.dev/platforms/tiktok)).
- **Postiz: no.** Both the hosted and self-hosted versions require **you** to register your own developer app with each platform and pass App Review/audit yourself — "there is no difference between the hosted version and the self-hosted version... both require you to set up your own OAuth applications with the platforms," and Postiz explicitly states it "can't do" the business verification/App Review for you ([analysis of Postiz self-hosting](https://jocheojeda.com/2026/06/16/what-is-postiz-self-hosting-social-scheduling/); [Postiz TikTok provider docs](https://docs.postiz.com/providers/tiktok)).
- **Mixpost: architecturally the same as Postiz** — an open-source scheduling layer you self-host on top of your own developer-app credentials, not a shared pre-approved app (UNVERIFIED in Mixpost-specific detail, but this is the standard pattern for self-hosted OSS schedulers vs. hosted SaaS wrappers).
- **Buffer / Hootsuite: not applicable as a bypass** — Buffer's third-party OAuth registration is currently closed to new developer apps entirely, and Hootsuite's API is Enterprise-gated with its own sales process; neither functions as an "instant multi-platform App-Review bypass" the way Ayrshare/Blotato do.

**This is the deciding factor for time-to-market**: Ayrshare, Blotato, and Late all let us skip Meta's 2–6 week Advanced Access review and TikTok's 1–2+ week audit entirely by riding on their pre-cleared apps. Postiz/Mixpost's much lower dollar cost is real, but it does not buy back that multi-week calendar-time cost — we would still be the ones filing for, and waiting on, App Review with those tools.

> - **Ayrshare bills per _profile_** — one profile = one brand/customer that can link **up to 13 social accounts across 13 networks**. Launch ($299) covers 10 profiles = **up to 130 linked accounts** ([Ayrshare pricing](https://www.ayrshare.com/pricing/)).
> - **Late, Postiz, Blotato bill per _connected account/channel_** — one Instagram login = one billable unit.
>
> **The table below uses the stricter, per-connected-account reading** (200 accounts = 200 billable units on Late/Postiz/Blotato = 200 profiles on Ayrshare). That is the correct reading **if** each account is a distinct persona/identity.
>
> **But if our 200 accounts are really ~15 brands × ~13 networks each**, Ayrshare collapses to ~15 profiles ≈ **$599/mo**, not $1,577 — and it becomes cost-competitive with Late rather than 4x worse. **This single modeling question materially changes the vendor ranking, and it depends on campaign structure we haven't fixed yet** (see "Open questions" below).

**Late is the cost-efficiency winner _under the per-connected-account reading_** ($48/$168/$418 at 10/50/200), and its formula is confirmed against the vendor's own published worked example. **This ranking is conditional, not absolute:** under the per-brand reading (few brands × many networks), Ayrshare's profile-based bundling closes most of the gap — 15 brands across 13 networks each = ~195 linked accounts for **$599/mo flat** on Business, versus roughly $400+/mo on Late's per-account card. Pick the vendor *after* fixing the account-structure question, not before.

Their low price buys a scheduling UI, not time-to-market. Judge them against "direct build plus a nice dashboard," not against Ayrshare/Late/Blotato.

## 7. Multi-account reality: legitimate SMM vs. platform-violating automation

**Meta.** "Inauthentic behavior" is defined as "a variety of complex forms of deception, performed by a network of inauthentic assets controlled by the same individual or individuals, with the goal of deceiving Meta or our community or to evade enforcement" ([Meta Transparency Center: Inauthentic Behavior](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/inauthentic-behavior/)). Meta states: "In line with our commitment to authenticity, we don't allow people to misrepresent themselves..., use fake accounts, [or] artificially boost the popularity of content" ([Meta Transparency Center: Inauthentic Behavior](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/inauthentic-behavior/)). The policy targets **fake/deceptive identity and coordinated networks pretending to be independent** — not a real brand publishing to its own, clearly-labeled official accounts via the official API, which is the exact use case the Content Publishing API is built for. Meta updated this policy on Dec 12, 2025 toward more content-agnostic framing ([search synthesis](https://www.platformgovernancearchive.org/2026/facebook-has-streamlined-its-community-guidelines-regarding-the-inauthentic-behaviour/)).

**TikTok.** Explicitly permits multiple accounts for legitimate purposes: "TikTok allows you to have multiple accounts—for example, for fan content or creative expression—but not to deceive others or break the rules" ([TikTok: Integrity and Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/integrity-authenticity)). Prohibited: "spam, including manipulating engagement signals to amplify the reach of certain content," and "fake engagement, such as... selling followers or likes" ([TikTok: Integrity and Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/integrity-authenticity)). Enforcement specifically looks for **coordination signals** — shared devices, shared technical fingerprints, accounts working together to spread identical narratives while pretending to be unrelated ([TikTok: Integrity and Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/integrity-authenticity)). A label openly operating its artist's own regional/language accounts is not the target; buying engagement or running accounts that impersonate independent fans is.

**X.** Has the most explicit rule directly on point for a multi-account marketing strategy: **"You may not post duplicative or substantially similar posts on one account or over multiple accounts you operate,"** and **"you may not create and/or automate multiple accounts for duplicative or substantially similar use cases."** Non-duplicative automation across owned accounts is explicitly fine — X's own example is automating regional accounts that each post *different* content (e.g., an ISS-tracker bot posting different city-pass alerts per account) ([secondary source quoting X's Automation Rules](https://opentweet.io/blog/twitter-automation-rules-2026); primary page `help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-automation` returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch — **treat exact wording as UNVERIFIED pending manual confirmation**, though the substance is corroborated by multiple independent secondary sources). **Practical implication:** posting the identical "Losing Sleep" clip to many X accounts we operate would likely violate this rule; content needs to be genuinely differentiated per account (different caption/edit/angle) to stay compliant.

**YouTube.** Prohibits "high-volume, repetitive, or deceptive comments... to drive traffic to or engagement with content," off-platform diversion content, and technical manipulation "designed to bypass abuse detection" ([YouTube: Spam, deceptive practices & scams policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973)). The policy explicitly states it "applies to... coordinated networks of channels" ([YouTube: Spam, deceptive practices & scams policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973)), and three strikes within 90 days can terminate a channel. Multiple official channels for different regions/languages are common industry practice and not targeted by this language; the target is deceptive engagement manipulation.

**Reddit.** As of the Nov 2025 "Responsible Builder Policy," self-service API access is closed entirely and even non-commercial access now requires pre-approval, a structural signal that Reddit has broadly tightened tolerance for automated activity of any kind, commercial or not ([Responsible Builder Policy — Reddit Help](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/42728983564564-Responsible-Builder-Policy)) — treat Reddit as high-risk for any automation-adjacent activity beyond manual, low-volume posting.

**Where the line actually sits, synthesized:** every platform researched draws the line at **deception** — fake identities, accounts pretending to be independent of each other, engagement manipulation (bought likes/follows/views), and content designed to evade detection systems — not at "a business uses software to publish to its own, clearly-labeled accounts." An artist/label running official, disclosed accounts through each platform's sanctioned publishing API is the paradigm case the APIs were built for. The genuine legal/policy risk in this project is narrower than "don't automate" — it is specifically: (1) don't disguise owned/branded accounts as independent fans, (2) don't buy or fabricate engagement, (3) on X specifically, don't post identical content across multiple owned accounts, and (4) expect and budget for TikTok's audit and Meta's Advanced-Access App Review if the plan ever extends to managing accounts on behalf of other artists/customers rather than only Respect the Funk's own.

## Open questions / what to resolve before committing budget

These are the unresolved items that most affect the build-vs-buy call. Each is a decision or a verification, not a research gap we can close from documentation alone.

1. **What *is* an "account" for this campaign?** (Blocks vendor selection.) Are we running **many distinct personas** (each its own IG/TikTok/YT identity) or **a few brands syndicated across many networks**? Per-account vendors (Late/Postiz/Blotato) win the first case; Ayrshare's per-profile bundling wins the second (§6). **This is a strategy decision, not a research finding** — and note the many-distinct-personas shape is also the one that attracts the most ToS scrutiny (§7), so it carries both a cost and a risk consequence.
2. **Instagram's real publishing ceiling: 100/24h or 50/24h?** Two official Meta pages disagree and we could not resolve it from docs (§1). Resolve empirically by querying `GET /{IG_ID}/content_publishing_limit` on a live account before sizing throughput.
3. **X's Automation Rules exact wording — UNVERIFIED.** The primary page (`help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-automation`) returns HTTP 403 to automated fetch; the "no duplicative posts across multiple accounts you operate" language is corroborated only via secondary sources (§7). **Confirm manually in a browser** — this rule directly constrains the multi-account strategy on X and deserves a first-party read.
4. **X media-upload billing under pay-per-use.** Whether each stage of the chunked INIT/APPEND/FINALIZE upload flow bills separately is **UNVERIFIED** — X's own dev community was asking this in 2026 with no official answer found. Since our content is video-first, this could move X's real cost well above the $0.015/post headline.
5. **TikTok audit lead time has no published SLA.** Third-party estimates run "days to weeks" (§2). Because this gates *any* direct TikTok posting, **file the audit application early and in parallel** with other work regardless of the build-vs-buy outcome — it is the long pole.
6. **Blotato's Agency-tier account cap and Snapchat Public Profile API's approval process** are both undocumented on the pages fetched (§5, §6) — require direct vendor contact.
