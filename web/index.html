What the research actually supports

Three findings we built the product around.

Not vibes. These are the load-bearing results from the literature, with the strongest available study designs — including the ones that constrain what we're allowed to claim.

Academic

Templatability is the mechanism — not audio quality.

Sounds spread when they imply a video a creator can copy, exposed through the "use this sound" repository. Hit-song science has failed repeatedly at the other explanation: 32,000 titles and 632 acoustic features could not learn popularity, and audio+lyrics explained only 1–8% of variance in a 646-entry study. The song is the substrate. The template is the product.

Academic

Network position predicts spread. The audio does not.

In hand-labeled TikTok virality analysis, creator follower count was the single strongest predictor and music was not significant at all. Which means the lever worth pulling is how many distinct follower graphs a release can enter — not how catchy the chorus is.

arXiv 2111.02452
(small-N: 400 hand-labeled videos)
Constraint

Breakout is lottery-shaped. So the edge is cost per attempt.

The best-designed cultural-markets experiment ever run — 14,341 participants, 48 songs, 8 parallel worlds — found quality only weakly predicts success, and that adding social influence increases both inequality and unpredictability. The top 10% of songs take 96% of all TikTok creations. When outcomes are lottery-shaped, the edge isn't a better ticket. It's more tickets at a radically lower cost per ticket.

Salganik, Dodds & Watts, Science 2006
arXiv 2405.14999 (quasi-experiment, UMG withdrawal)

How it works

Create. Test. Measure. Repeat on what survived.

One loop, run per release, against your actual roster — with the expensive step amortised across every song an artist ever puts out.

01 — Create

Templates, not finished ads.

Register an artist once — look, wardrobe, likeness consent. After that every song generates a pack of vertical, remix-ready templates in minutes, built to be imitated rather than admired. Multiple variants per release, deliberately, because you don't know which shape lands.

02 — Test

Through accounts we openly own.

Variants go out through a small network of disclosed, label-owned niche accounts — never covert, never impersonating fans. This is the transparent version of a tactic the industry mostly runs in the dark, and it produces the one thing seeding never does: a clean read on which template shape earns pickup.

03 — Measure

Only what's genuinely countable.

Sound-usage data is measured behaviour, not inferred demographics. Clicks are real. We report those, per variant, per release — and we refuse to report the numbers this industry routinely invents.

The compounding part: the artist identity is built once and reused forever, so the second release is materially cheaper than the first — and every test result carries forward into what we generate next.
Testing through our networks

Disclosed accounts. Not simulated trends.

Every platform's rule reduces to one test: does the account deceive people about who is behind it? Not how many accounts you run. Meta and TikTok both ship first-class infrastructure for one business operating many accounts — it's the deception that's banned, not the count.

So we run the version that's openly the label's. The industry's cautionary tale here — an agency that admitted on record to "posting enough volume across enough accounts to simulate the idea that the song is trending" — didn't get banned. It got found out, and an analyst who checked its accounts found poor engagement metrics anyway. The covert version may not even work, and you'll be hated for trying.

Honest scoping: this is a slow instrument. Account tiers under 5K followers now average ~350 views per post, down 59% year over year, and a usable audience signal realistically takes 3–6 months per account. We start small and treat it as a capability test, not a growth engine. Anyone promising you faster is guessing.

Analytics

We report what's real and refuse the rest.

There is no conversion signal flowing from Spotify back to any ad platform — tracking ends at the click. Every vendor reporting streams-per-dollar is modelling, and most won't tell you that. We'd rather hand you three honest numbers than twelve invented ones.

The same applies to creator demographics. No platform exposes an arbitrary creator's follower breakdown — third-party tools infer it from a visible sample, and not one vendor in that market publishes an accuracy figure. Sound-usage data is measured behaviour and roughly 10× cheaper. We use that instead.

Plus a full cost ledger: provider, model, units, cost — per kit and per delivered asset, exported warehouse-ready. Nobody else in this category shows you the bill.

The plumbing

Two real levers, both free, both usually left on the floor.

Before anything clever happens, the boring things have to be right — and in our research these were the only tactical levers that survived scrutiny at zero cost.

$0

Set the TikTok clip window to the hook.

At distribution you specify a 60-second window creators pick from. Leave it blank and the default is the first 60 seconds — chosen algorithmically, not hook-aware. It's a coarse lever: you set the menu, not the meal. But because "tap to use" tends to grab from the window's start, setting it at the hook biases the median creator. Confirmed across DistroKid, TuneCore, CD Baby and SoundOn — support and editability differ, so we check yours.

$0

Fix Sound ID fragmentation before release.

Industry analysis found 2.8 million Sound IDs across 141,000 songs — roughly 20× fragmentation. A track that breaks out on the wrong Sound ID earns nothing. Metadata hygiene is unglamorous, costs nothing, and is the difference between a windfall and a windfall someone else banks.

Virality guarantees

Identical songs in identical experimental worlds produced wildly different winners. Anyone selling you a breakout is selling the ticket, not the odds.

Paid seeding

No rigorous evidence it causes durable organic pickup, credible testimony against it, and a live reputational case study. We don't buy plays, followers, engagement or accounts.

Stream-count attribution

Technically impossible — the signal doesn't exist. Any dashboard showing it is modelling and calling it measurement.
